# Phishing Attack Patterns and Detection Indicators

## Overview
Phishing is a deceptive attempt by malicious actors to acquire sensitive information such as usernames, passwords, credit card numbers, and banking credentials by masquerading as a trustworthy entity in an electronic communication.

## Key Indicators
1. **Urgent or Coercive Language**: Threats of immediate account closure, legal prosecution, or service suspension within a tight timeframe (e.g., "within 24 hours", "immediate action required").
2. **Deceptive Call to Action**: Unsolicited instructions to click a verification link, download an attachment, or reply with confidential details.
3. **Mismatched Sender Identity**: Domains that visually resemble reputable brands using typosquatting or strange Top-Level Domains (TLDs).
4. **Generic or Manipulative Greetings**: Impersonal salutations accompanied by severe warnings.
5. **Fake Security or KYC Verification**: Demands to re-verify Know Your Customer (KYC) documentation, tax forms, or digital identity proofs.

## Defensive Mitigations
- Do not follow links provided in unsolicited electronic messages.
- Manually navigate to official websites via verified bookmarks or official mobile applications.
- Inspect full sender headers and domain names for anomalies.
- Never submit credentials or one-time passcodes through third-party forms.
